FlashAttention across generations, cycle by cycle

We ran Dao-AILab's FlashAttention-2 and FlashAttention-4 on an A100 and a B300 and recorded the exact instruction path of every warp. We then replayed those paths in a cycle-level simulator. The simulator matches Nsight Compute to within 6% on all sixteen kernels we validated, so we can use it to ask what limits each generation and how long-context attention behaves on 72 GPUs.

What we measured

Forward attention, BF16, head dimension 128, 16 heads, 16k tokens per batch (batch × sequence), non-causal. Implementations: naive PyTorch (QKᵀ, softmax, PV as three kernels), PyTorch SDPA with its bundled FlashAttention-2 and with cuDNN, Dao-AILab flash_attn 2 (CUDA, built from source), and FlashAttention-4 (flash_attn/cute, CuTe DSL, JIT-compiled). FA3 needs a Hopper GPU, which this study did not have. Timing is back-to-back calls between CUDA events, so host overhead is hidden and the GPU runs at its sustained clock. The A100 PCIe panel is the one exception: it was timed one call per event pair. Kernel cycles come from Nsight Compute with caches flushed.

TFLOP/s at fixed 16k tokens. On A100, FA2, FA4's Sm80 path, and PyTorch's FA2 land within 15% of each other near 63–73% of the 312 TF/s peak. On B300, FA2 stays near 0.37 PF/s while FA4 and cuDNN sustain 1.4–1.7 PF/s. Naive attention stays below 80 TF/s on both because it writes S and P to HBM. Run back to back, the B300 delivers less than on a single call. FA4 at 4k drops from 1.88 to 1.52 PF/s, consistent with the power-limited clock below.

The B300 runs FA2 on a different tensor core

FA2 issues mma.sync (HMMA m16n8k16) from every warp. Blackwell keeps that instruction, but our calibration measures it at 2037 FLOP/clk/SM, the same per-SM rate as an A100 and a quarter of tcgen05.mma (8192 FLOP/clk/SM). FA2 on B300 is an A100 kernel on a wider, faster-clocked chip, held to that legacy ceiling. Nothing in FA2's design is wrong for Blackwell: the hardware moved the fast path to an asynchronous, single-thread-issued MMA with its accumulator in tensor memory, and reaching it needs FA4's structure.

B300 drops its clock under FA4

Nsight Compute counted 510 k SM cycles in FA4's 290 µs, an effective 1.76 GHz against the 2.03 GHz the chip holds on lighter kernels. FA4 keeps the tensor core busy enough that the B300 runs at a power-limited clock, and the sustained sweep loses another few percent to it. All comparisons below are in cycles, which removes the clock from the comparison.

How the simulator sees a warp-specialised kernel

FA4 on Blackwell splits each CTA into roles. A load warp issues TMA copies, one warp issues 2-CTA tcgen05.mma, two softmax warpgroups read S from tensor memory and write P back, a correction warpgroup rescales O, and an epilogue warp stores the result. The roles hand work to each other through mbarriers and named barriers, and the CTA pair shares barriers across two SMs.

A static listing with a branch profile cannot reproduce this: every role takes a different path through the same code. So we recorded the paths. A new NVBit tool, warp_path, records every instruction each warp of a few CTAs executed, together with what a timing model needs and SASS alone does not give: the mbarrier behind each SYNCS op, expect-tx byte counts, the outcome and parity of every try_wait, the source box of every TMA copy (for all CTAs), UMMA instruction descriptors, and the registers that name barriers. It also saves the 128-bit instruction encodings, so the simulator uses the compiler's own stall counts and scoreboards.

The converter removes spin loops (a failed try_wait, the sleep and the retries). The simulator then re-times the pipeline itself rather than replaying the spins the hardware happened to do. Each successful wait becomes an absolute phase count. A repeated parity means the warp skipped a phase, which FA4's lazy rescaling does on purpose. We confirmed each case against the hardware clock: exactly two commits landed between such waits.

To make replay work, the simulator gained mbarrier transaction counts, cross-SM barriers for CTA pairs (at the measured 193-cycle DSMEM latency), 2-CTA MMAs on both SMs' tensor cores, register-named and generation-ordered named barriers, a separate mma.sync rate, and stall attribution per warp role and per barrier.

All sixteen kernels are inside the ±10% band. The 1024-token FA4 kernels last only 32 µs, and they first came out 17–21% fast: the simulator was missing a fixed per-kernel cost (cluster launch, tensor-memory allocation, mbarrier setup, pipeline fill and drain). The calibration suite now measures that window on almost-empty kernels of the same launch shape under ncu, and the simulator charges it along each warp's own exit path, with a separately fitted tcgen05.alloc latency (1430 cycles for one CTA, 1990 for a pair). Those were fitted on synthetic prologue kernels, so FA4 is an out-of-sample check: its 1024-token steps now land at −3.7% and −2.7%.

Where FA4's cycles go on B300

Per-role warp-cycles from the replay. Warp 12 includes the pair's peer CTA, whose MMA warp issues nothing and waits at a named barrier. In the leader CTA the tensor core is busy 91% of the kernel.

The softmax warpgroups spend about 40% of their cycles waiting for the tensor core to finish S, and most of the rest in fixed-latency dependencies of the exp/scale/pack chain. The math pipes (MUFU.EX2, FFMA2) are rarely the reason a softmax warp stalls. The correction warpgroup is idle almost all the time, because lazy rescaling skips most of its work. The MMA warp is fed: on B300 the kernel is tensor-bound, with softmax latency on the critical path between the two GEMMs of each step.

One hardware number at a time

Because the kernel is replayed from its real instructions, we can change one measured number in the calibration file and see what it does to this exact kernel.

  • Exp unit at the B200 rate: FA4's B300 kernel runs at 0.79× when MUFU.EX2 issues at 16/clk/SM instead of the 32 we measured on B300. This is the bottleneck FA4 works around on B200 by computing part of exp2 on the FMA pipe. In FA4's tuning table the sm_103 configurations set ex2_emu_freq = 0, and our replay agrees: doubling EX2 again gains 1.00×.
  • A faster tensor core alone stops paying early: 1.5× the tcgen05 rate gives 1.21×, and 2× gives the same 1.21×. At 2× the exp unit is the limit again. Doubling MUFU.EX2 as well takes the kernel to 1.51×, while doubling the FMA pipe instead gives only 1.24×. B300 bought FA4 one generation of exp headroom by doubling EX2. A part that doubles tensor throughput again without doubling EX2 brings back the B200 situation, and with it the case for FA4's exp emulation.
  • HBM bandwidth does not matter here: halving it changes FA4 by 1.00×. At 4k context the kernel reuses K and V from L2 (87% hit rate in the replay) and moves only the 201 MB ncu also counts.
  • FA2's exp is not its problem either: on both GPUs, FA2 changes by at most 2% with the exp rate halved or doubled. It is bound by HMMA issue and its dependency chains. Removing the A100's measured HMMA/FP32-pipe coupling gives 1.01×.

Long context on 72 GPUs

For prefill at long context, attention is split by context parallelism. Each GPU owns S/N tokens and runs N ring steps. Each step runs the validated kernel on its queries against one chunk of keys, while the next chunk of K and V moves to the neighbouring GPU (double buffered). The model is Llama-3-70B attention (64 query heads, head dim 128), with grouped-query (8 KV heads) and full multi-head (64 KV heads) variants. Every kernel is the replayed FA4 (B300) or FA2 (A100) kernel, and every transfer is simulated packet by packet on the fabric. The fabrics are not calibrated yet, so these results are model behaviour for the network.

Limits

  • FA3 (Hopper) is not in this study; Della's H100 partitions were not available to this account, and the GH200 queue started the next day.
  • The recordings sample four CTAs. Other CTAs replay a recorded CTA of the same cluster rank, with their real TMA sources (FA4) or per-instruction address strides (FA2). Persistent kernels whose CTAs process one tile fewer replay one tile too many, which adds 2–15% instructions on the smallest shapes.
  • The fixed kernel window is measured on near-empty kernels of the same launch shape, not on FA4 itself. The rack runs below predate it and charge only the launch gap, which matters only for the 1024-token steps.
  • FA4's Python dispatch costs about 50–80 µs per call on top of a 32 µs kernel at the 1024-token ring shape. A real ring would capture the steps in a CUDA graph; the rack runs launch them as a graph would.
  • NVLink and InfiniBand numbers come from presets, not from a multi-GPU calibration.
