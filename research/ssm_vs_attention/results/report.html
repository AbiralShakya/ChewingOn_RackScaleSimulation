Mamba-2 vs attention on Blackwell

Measured on a real B300 and A100, explained instruction by instruction, then scaled to a 72-GPU rack in the simulator. Blackwell pushes the point where Mamba-2 beats attention to roughly three times longer sequences than on Ampere, because FlashAttention-4 rides the new tensor cores and HBM while the Mamba-2 kernels do not.

Setup

Three 8B-class models share d_model 4096 and Llama 3's vocabulary: a Transformer with the Llama-3-8B shape (32 query heads, 8 KV heads, head dim 128), a pure Mamba-2 stack (expand 2, 128 heads of 64, d_state 128, 8 groups, chunk 256; 64 blocks so its 7.0B non-embedding parameters match the Transformer's), and the hybrid layout of Waleffe et al. (2024): 24 Mamba-2, 4 attention and 28 MLP blocks. The rack-scale runs use 70B-class versions of the same three at d_model 8192, each with about 68.5B non-embedding parameters.

Every kernel comes from the open-source implementations: selective_state_update and mamba_chunk_scan_combined from state-spaces/mamba 2.3.2 (Triton), causal_conv1d 1.7.0, FlashAttention-4 (CuTe DSL) on B300 and FlashAttention-2 2.8.3 on A100, and cuBLAS for the projections. Each kernel was timed on a B300 SXM6 (della-tridao, GPU 3) and an A100 80GB PCIe (Della) with CUDA graphs and a cold L2, then profiled with Nsight Compute for cycles, instruction counts, DRAM bytes and stall reasons, and its SASS was dumped. The simulator then runs the same shapes on its calibrated B300 and A100 models (the measured clocks, latencies, tensor-core and mma.sync rates from calibration/). Beyond one GPU, only the simulator is used.

1. Decode: the state update is issue-bound

At decode time an attention layer reads its KV cache, which grows with context; a Mamba-2 layer reads and writes a fixed recurrent state (2 MB per sequence per layer in bf16). On paper the state wins once the KV cache is larger than twice the state. On B300 it takes much longer than that.

selective_state_update moves the state at only 2.0 TB/s on B300, about a quarter of its 7.7 TB/s, while FlashAttention-4 streams the KV cache at up to 6.9 TB/s. On A100 the state update reaches 1.0 TB/s, more than half of the part's bandwidth. That gap is why the crossover moves from 1.1k tokens on A100 to 3.3k on B300.

The cause is visible in the SASS. With d_state 128 the kernel's hand-tuned table picks BLOCK_SIZE_M = 4 and 4 warps, so each CTA owns 4 rows of headdim and moves just 2 KB of state. The compiled kernel (sm_103a, Triton 3.8) is about 270 instructions, mostly address arithmetic, an integer division for the head-to-group mapping, five BAR.SYNCs and an STSM/LDS round trip that broadcasts B and C through shared memory. Nsight Compute counts 253 instructions per warp and reports the warps as mostly not selected and math pipe throttle: the SMs are busy issuing instructions, and the DRAM pipe sits at 11 to 25% utilisation. On A100 the same work is a smaller multiple of the time HBM needs, so the kernel looks bandwidth-bound there. A larger BLOCK_SIZE_M or a fused state update with the out projection would recover most of the gap; the simulator runs the real Triton SASS for this kernel, so it can test such a change before it is written.

2. Prefill: FlashAttention-4 got faster than SSD did

Moving from A100 to B300, causal FlashAttention at 16k tokens gets 7.8× faster, but the Mamba-2 SSD scan gets only 3.1× faster, so the prefill crossover moves from 4.1k to 10.9k tokens. The SSD is five Triton kernels, and none of them is a large, dense GEMM that can keep tcgen05 busy:

  • chunk_scan is 64% of the SSD on B300. The autotuner settles on a 2-warp, 64x32 tile; each CTA issues a few small UTCHMMAs per k-step and spends the rest on the decay mask exp(dA_i - dA_j): one MUFU.EX2 per element of the 256x256 C B^T block, plus the SiLU gate. Tensor pipes stay mostly idle, which is the inverse of FlashAttention-4, which keeps two 128-row Q tiles in flight to hide its exponentials behind the MMAs.
  • chunk_cumsum costs a flat 38 µs whatever the length: 32 to 128 CTAs on 148 SMs, one warp per scheduler, 7617 instructions per warp of fully unrolled scan, stalled on instruction fetch. At 4k tokens that is 12% of the SSD.
  • state_passing is a sequential scan over chunks; every iteration waits on a DRAM load (long scoreboard), so it is latency-bound on both parts.

3. GB200 NVL72: what the rack sees

The 70B-class models run as tensor-parallel replicas inside one NVL72 domain. For every model, context length and batch the simulator reports the decode step at cycle level: weights streamed per GPU, the attention or Mamba-2 kernels, the NVLink all-reduces, and the launch gaps of a CUDA-graph replay. Each batch is also checked against the GPU's 186 GB of HBM (90% usable), so the throughput below is the best batch that actually fits.

TP8 on GB200 NVL72, best batch from 1 to 512 that fits in HBM. Bold is the fastest model at that context.

  • Short contexts belong to the Transformer. At 1k tokens it decodes 3,637 tokens/s per GPU, 1.4× the hybrid and 3.0× pure Mamba-2: the KV cache is small and FA4 reads it near HBM speed, while every Mamba-2 layer pays the issue-bound state update.
  • From 16k tokens the hybrid wins (2,006 vs 700 tokens/s per GPU). With 9 attention layers instead of 80 its KV cache is a ninth of the Transformer's, so it keeps batch 512 in HBM where the Transformer drops to 128.
  • Pure Mamba-2 is flat at 1,196 tokens/s per GPU at every context, because its cost per token does not depend on context. It passes the hybrid only at 128k tokens. Its ceiling is the state update: at batch 512 a TP8 decode step reads and writes about 88 GB of state per GPU, at under 2 TB/s.
  • What-if: a state update with 8 headdim rows per CTA instead of 4 (same math, twice the bytes per CTA, half the CTAs) lifts pure Mamba-2 to 2,310 tokens/s per GPU at TP8 (1.9×), ahead of the Transformer from about 4k tokens and of the hybrid from about 8k. The simulator lets this kernel stream at about 7.5 TB/s, above the 6.4 TB/s copy bandwidth measured on B300, so read it as an upper bound that is about 15% high.

The rack sweeps run per-GPU shapes that differ from the 8B ones (TP8 leaves 8 query heads and 1 KV head per GPU for attention, 32 heads and 1 group for Mamba-2, and narrower projections), so those were measured on the B300 too: 33 cases, median error 11%, and 10% for the state updates and attention kernels that decide the rack results.

Two references are reported: the CUDA-graph timing, and Nsight Compute's per-kernel duration, which replays one kernel at a time. Under Nsight Compute the tensor-bound kernels already run at about 1.79 GHz on the B300 (2.0 GHz for the memory-bound ones); in a sustained back-to-back replay the power cap pulls the clock lower still, and the large GEMMs take 15 to 30% longer than Nsight Compute reports. The simulator's large-GEMM times sit close to the Nsight Compute numbers.

What it took to get there, all on this branch: FlashAttention-4's two-Q-tile ping-pong on Blackwell; HMMAs with independent accumulators and double-buffered ldmatrix fragments (the synthetic kernels had chained every HMMA and every exponential through one register); split-K and narrow N tiles for decode-sized GEMMs, as cuBLAS uses; the autotuned SSD tile shapes and the L2 reuse of C B^T across heads; a separate, measured mma.sync rate (on B300 it is 2037 BF16 FLOP/clk/SM, a quarter of tcgen05); and the real Triton SASS for the state update.

The A100 model is the weaker of the two. Its synthetic mma.sync GEMMs land a median 33% from cuBLAS, and it runs the SSD scan 60 to 70% slower than the hardware, almost all of it in chunk_scan. The crossovers above come from the hardware measurements, and the rack results use only the Blackwell model, so neither depends on the A100 simulation. Small kernels under about 10 µs (launch-bound conv1d updates, the split-KV combine) are the other known gap on both parts.

Reproduce

# on the GPU (B300: della-tridao GPU 3; A100: Della Slurm)
research/ssm_vs_attention/hw/setup_env.sh          # torch, mamba_ssm, causal-conv1d, FA4/FA2
python research/ssm_vs_attention/hw/bench_kernels.py --out timing.json
research/ssm_vs_attention/hw/run_ncu.sh out/        # Nsight Compute + SASS

# anywhere (simulator)
maturin develop --release
python research/ssm_vs_attention/validate.py --gpu b300
python research/ssm_vs_attention/sweep.py --family 70b --full-rack
python research/ssm_vs_attention/analyze.py && python research/ssm_vs_attention/build_report.py

Raw data: research/ssm_vs_attention/data/ (timings, Nsight Compute CSVs, Triton SASS and metadata); results: results/*.json. The MFU over-count of the LM head for prefill and training with --layers is fixed on the same branch.
